### FINDINGS

#### 1. The "content-free" proxy is an ITEM-LENGTH proxy, so the headline 4.7% → 37.2% measures string length, not subject matter

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/trial_pool_unit.py:187`, `:246`, `:353`;
  `Brain/calibration/trial_pool_unit_gemini.py:225`, `:290`. Both call
  `cluster_evidence.is_substantive(text, 5)` (`Brain/shared/cluster_evidence.py:200-213`).
- **What is wrong**: `is_substantive` is an **absolute** count of ≥5 non-stop alphabetic tokens
  in ONE pool item. The clause arm's items are single sentences; the turn arm's items are whole
  turns, i.e. concatenations of ~3 clauses. Concatenation SUMS content words, so an item that is
  content-free as a turn implies every constituent clause was content-free, but not conversely.
  The proxy therefore cannot report a higher content-free rate for turns than for clauses on the
  same text — it is a metric that can only move one way when the unit changes (taxonomy item 5),
  and the two arms differ on exactly the property the threshold is sensitive to (item 1).
  `thin_fraction < 0.30` ("subject-bearing") and `>= 0.70` ("junk") inherit this directly.
- **Measured from the artifacts, and it is decisive.** Mean words per sampled cluster member is
  11.5 (clause) vs 28.7 (turn), a 2.5x gap. `corr(thin_fraction, mean sample words)` is **-0.787**
  (clause) and **-0.839** (turn). Binned by mean item length, the two arms are the same metric:

  | mean sample words | clause: n, subject-share, mean thin | turn: n, subject-share, mean thin |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 0–8    | 60, 0.0%, 0.91 | 67, 0.0%, 0.86 |
  | 8–12   | 41, 0.0%, 0.73 | 29, 0.0%, 0.69 |
  | 12–18  | 47, 0.0%, 0.56 | 18, 11.1%, 0.54 |
  | 18–26  | 16, 31.2%, 0.43 | 13, 53.8%, 0.31 |
  | 26–40  | 6, 33.3%, 0.32 | 11, 81.8%, 0.17 |
  | 40+    | 1, 100%, 0.28 | **53**, 100%, 0.06 |

  Within every band the arms are close; the entire 4.7% → 37.2% gap comes from the turn arm having
  53 clusters with mean item length ≥40 words against the clause arm's 1. Note also that the turn
  arm has **more** ≤8-word clusters than the clause arm (67 vs 60) — turn mode did not remove the
  backchannel families, it added long clusters beside them.
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: inflates the turn arm's
  `subject_bearing`/`subject_share` and deflates the clause arm's. It corrupts the two most-cited
  numbers of this whole effort — "subject-bearing clusters 8 (4.7%) clause vs 71 (37.2%) turn" and
  "content-free 59.1% vs 32.8%" — and the derived "71 coherent scenarios" reading. The
  *direction* of the underlying conclusion may still be right (whole turns do carry more subject
  matter), but the magnitude is unsupported and "37.2% are subject-bearing" is not a statement
  about content: a long string passes the 5-content-word bar automatically.
- **Confidence**: certain (the mechanism is a code fact; the length-matched table is arithmetic
  from the shipped artifacts).
- **Cheapest way to verify**: the python snippet above over
  `artifacts/pool_unit_clause.json` and `artifacts/pool_unit_turn_092.json`, no pipeline run
  needed. A unit-normalised replacement (e.g. content-word share per 100 words, or the threshold
  scaled by each arm's mean item length) can be computed from the same artifacts for free.

#### 3. "3072 beats 768 for clustering" compares `subject_share` at equal merge threshold, not at equal cluster count — and `subject_share` rises steeply with cluster count

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/trial_pool_unit_gemini.py:266-302` (the sweep loop and its
  `subject_share = len(sb)/d` denominator).
- **What is wrong**: `subject_share` is a share of *surviving clusters*, and finer clustering makes
  each cluster more homogeneous, so the metric increases monotonically with cluster count within a
  single width. At a fixed merge threshold the two widths do NOT produce the same number of
  surviving clusters, so the widths are compared at different points on that curve (item 1).
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: inflates 3072's apparent advantage.
  Within 3072 the curve is surviving → subject_share: 90→0.200, 136→0.265, 183→0.317, 217→0.369,
  230→0.387, 245→0.396 (≈ +0.0013 per cluster). Reading the two widths at *matched surviving
  counts* instead of matched thresholds: 183 vs 193 → 0.317 vs 0.321 (**768 ahead**);
  217 vs 209 → 0.369 vs 0.335; 230 vs 225 → 0.387 vs 0.360; 245 vs 234 → 0.396 vs 0.368.
  Interpolating 3072's own curve to 768's cluster counts, the gap at 0.92 (0.265 vs 0.229) vanishes
  entirely and the gap at the top shrinks from ~3.6pt to ~2.2pt. So "3072 wins at every merge
  threshold from 0.92 up" is partly an artifact; the honest statement is "3072 wins at the coarse
  end only after controlling for cluster count, and ties or loses at the fine end".
- **Confidence**: certain (pure arithmetic from `pool_unit_gemini_full_3072d.json` and
  `..._768d.json`).
- **Cheapest way to verify**: read the two `sweep` arrays side by side and align rows on
  `surviving` rather than `threshold`. No re-embedding needed — both artifacts are already paid for.

#### 13. `scenario_coherence`'s GO/NO-GO gate is a test that the distribution has spread, not that the split is meaningful — and coherence is uncontrolled for scenario size

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/scenario_coherence.py:61-73` (`coherence`), `:120-131`
  (median split, half means, `separation`), `:149-155` (the gate).
- **What is wrong**: two compounding issues. (a) Mean-cosine-to-centroid falls systematically as
  member count rises, and nothing here size-normalises it — the sibling `trial_pool_unit.py`
  builds an explicit size-matched null for exactly this reason (`:120-131`), and this file does
  not. So "loose half" is partly "large scenarios" and "tight half" partly "small scenarios"
  (item 6). (b) The gate splits a continuous distribution at its own median and asks whether the
  two half-means differ by ≥0.05; for any distribution with an interquartile range above ~0.05
  that is satisfied by construction, regardless of whether the split corresponds to anything
  (item 5 — ask what value the metric takes in the degenerate case: a pure-noise coherence
  distribution with the observed spread also passes).
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: the `separation` figure and the GO verdict
  that licenses spending the paid discrimination half. Inflates the apparent separation by the
  amount attributable to scenario size, and the gate cannot return NO-GO for a spread-but-
  meaningless split.
- **Confidence**: likely on (a) — I did not measure `corr(coherence, n_triggers)` because it needs
  a DB read; certain on (b) as a property of a median split.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: `artifacts/scenario_coherence.json` stores `n_triggers` and
  `trigger_coherence` per row — `np.corrcoef` over those two columns settles (a) for free, and
  `np.percentile(tc, [25,75])` settles whether the 0.05 bar is reachable-but-not-guaranteed.

#### 14. TEST 2's sample is unseeded and length-filtered

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/validate_taxonomy_vs_layerd.py:142-145`
  (`... AND length(trim(trigger_text)) > 20 ORDER BY random() LIMIT 600`).
- **What is wrong**: `ORDER BY random()` with no `setseed`, so the 600 turns differ every run and
  the printed rates are not reproducible; and `length > 20` is a **character** filter that removes
  short client turns — precisely the population where the sink-rejection mechanism decides the
  outcome (items 13 and 2). Both taxonomies see the same 600, so the comparison itself stays
  symmetric.
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: **inflates both** keep rates
  ("85.3% vs 74.0%"), since longer turns are more likely to match a coachable entry — so these are
  not coverage estimates over Layer D's real moments. The 11.3-point gap between the arms is the
  only part of TEST 2 that is defensible, and it still carries the ground-truth circularity the
  docstring already admits (the moments are defined by production's own taxonomy).
- **Confidence**: certain.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: read the SQL at `:142`; add `SELECT setseed(0.42);` before it and
  run twice to see the rates move.
