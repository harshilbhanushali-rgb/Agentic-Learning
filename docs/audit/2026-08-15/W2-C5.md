#### 3. The report's headline coachable rate uses a denominator production never has, printed next to a production reference

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/trial_adjudicate_gemini.py:136-144`
  (`coach = kinds["scenario"]` … `f"coachable: {coach}/{len(rows)}   [live production for reference: 85/161 = 52.8%]"`).
- **What is wrong**: two independent errors on one line. (a) The numerator excludes merged
  clusters (finding 1's collapse). (b) The **denominator** `len(rows)` counts all 245 adjudicated
  clusters including the 69 merged ones — but in production a `merge_into` cluster produces **no
  row at all** (`v2/layer_a.py:529 continue`), so production's 161 is the analogue of 245 − 69 =
  176, not 245.
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: **deflates** turn mode's coachable rate
  against the production reference sitting on the same line. Printed 38/245 = **15.5%**;
  production-comparable is 38/176 = **21.6%** (turn-aware run: 17.6% printed vs 23.9%
  comparable). Separately, the 52.8% reference is confounded on at least five further axes —
  embedder (bge vs gemini-embedding-2@3072), pool unit (clause vs turn), `min_cluster_size`
  (50-of-73,771 vs 16-of-23,949), merge threshold (0.85 vs 0.97) and adjudicating model
  (`gemma-4-31b-it` vs `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`). The file's own `--min-cluster-size` help text
  warns against exactly one of those five and then prints the comparison anyway.
- **Confidence**: certain for (a) and (b); the confound list is certain from CLAUDE.md's own
  provenance notes.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: `python -c` over `artifacts/adjudicate_gemini_min16.json` counting

- **What is wrong**: `tot_turns` is 12,582 — the turns that survived both HDBSCAN (noise_share

- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: **inflates every turn-share by ~1.9x**.
  "Retention 41.0% of turns" is 5,159/12,582 → **21.5%** of the corpus. "coachable turn volume

- **Confidence**: certain (denominators recomputed from the artifacts).
- **Cheapest way to verify**: `sum(r["n_items"] for r in rows)` = 12,582 against

#### 5. The min-16-vs-min-50 head-to-head conclusions do not survive a naive two-proportion test

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/aggregate_cluster_verdicts.py:119-135` (the HEAD TO HEAD table).
- **What is wrong**: the table prints bare percentages with no interval and no test, and the
  published conclusion ("finer granularity is BETTER: coherence 84% vs 75%, coachability 38% vs
  33%") is drawn from them. Computed: coherent 207/245 vs 57/76 → +9.5pp, SE 5.5pp, **z = 1.73**;
  coachable 92/245 vs 25/76 → +4.7pp, SE 6.2pp, **z = 0.75**. Neither clears p<0.05 *before*
  accounting for the fact that the two arms partition the **same corpus** — the min-50 clusters are
  near-unions of min-16 ones, so the arms are not independent samples and the true effective n is
  smaller than 245/76 (taxonomy item 14). Separately, `judge_coherent` is `coh["yes"]` only
  (`:114`), silently binning the 24 min-16 / 12 min-50 `partial` verdicts as failures — a third
  enum collapse; scoring partial as 0.5 gives 89.4% vs 82.9%, same direction, smaller gap.
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: converts two null results into a stated
  finding. Direction of the point estimates is preserved; their **significance is fabricated by
  omission**.
- **Confidence**: certain for the arithmetic; "likely" that the dependence shrinks it further
  (not modelled).
- **Cheapest way to verify**: the four counts are in `artifacts/cluster_verdict_summary.json`;
  a two-proportion z is one line.

#### 6. Gemma and the blind judges were shown different evidence, so their agreement rate is not a clean measure of either

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/trial_adjudicate_gemini.py:285` (`c["texts"][:REPRESENTATIVE_SHOWN]`,
  i.e. the first **6**, untruncated) vs `Brain/calibration/export_cluster_batches.py:91-93`
  (**12** samples, strided across the cluster, each hard-truncated to **260 characters**).
- **What is wrong**: taxonomy item 1. Three properties differ besides the judge: sample count
  (6 vs 12), selection rule (head vs stride — and for the 78 of 245 clusters with `n_items < 24`
  the stride is 1, so those judges also got the head, contrary to the comment's claim), and
  truncation. **632 of 2,940 judge samples (21.5%) were cut at 260 chars**, keeping the *opening*
  of the turn — which is precisely the part the `--turn-aware` note says is filler
  ("a substantive turn routinely OPENS with acknowledgement before its real content").
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: the judge-vs-Gemma agreement rate
  (finding 1's number) and the standalone judge coachability rate. Truncation biases the judges
  toward "not coachable", i.e. **deflates** judge-coachable (92/245); the extra 6 samples push
  the other way. Net direction unresolved, which is the problem — the agreement number blends a
  granularity effect with an evidence-presentation effect.
- **Confidence**: certain that the arms differ; likely on the net magnitude.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: count samples of length exactly 260 in
  `artifacts/cluster_batches/min16_batch*.json` (632), and compare `SAMPLES_PER_CLUSTER = 12`
  against `REPRESENTATIVE_SHOWN = 6`.

#### 7. The "verified position-for-position" join guard has power over only 18% of rows

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/export_cluster_batches.py:124-132`.
- **What is wrong**: the guard compares `n_items` at each position and claims a silent mismatch is
  impossible. **200 of the 245 rows share their `n_items` value with at least one other row**
  (largest tie: 15 clusters at 18 turns; 12 at 16; 11 at 21), so any permutation *within* a size
  tie passes the check and attaches one cluster's utterances to another cluster's verdict — the
  exact failure the comment says it prevents. **188 of 245 rows sit in a tie group containing more
  than one `kind`**, so such a swap would move the agreement number. `st.distinct_calls` is already
  computed at `:84` and would cut the tied population from 200 to **76** at zero cost; the c-TF-IDF
  keywords (also available, `tm` is returned at `:66`) would cut it further.
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: latent. If ordering ever diverges, every
  judge-derived number (agreement, judge coachable/coherent, the proxy second-opinion) is
  corrupted in an unpredictable direction with no error raised. There is no evidence it diverged
  in the recorded runs (gemini embeddings are deterministic), so this is a guard-strength defect,
  not a demonstrated miscount.
- **Confidence**: certain about the guard's weakness; speculative that it has ever bitten.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: `collections.Counter(r["n_items"] for r in rows)` over
  `artifacts/adjudicate_gemini_min16.json`.

#### 9. The harness's merge handling diverges from the production code it claims to be a transport-only copy of

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/trial_adjudicate_gemini.py:300-319` vs
  `Brain/v2/layer_a.py:504-529`.
- **What is wrong**: the docstring says "TRANSPORT ONLY … Only the transport differs". Two
  behavioural differences. (a) On `merge_into`, production re-centres the target's centroid on the
  **union** (`layer_a.py:523-527`) and grows its `support_calls`/`support_clauses`; the harness
  does neither — merged evidence never reaches the accepted-list, so the "NEAREST SCENARIOS
  ALREADY ACCEPTED" block the whole sequential design exists to feed is built from unblended
  first-member centroids. (b) Production validates `merge_into_key` against `by_key` and demotes an
  unmatchable key to `new_scenario` with a printed warning (`layer_a.py:507-514`); the harness
  accepts any string, so a hallucinated target would be counted as a retained duplicate.
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: (a) biases the merge count — production's
  wider union basins should attract *more* merges, so the harness likely **under-counts**
  duplicate detection (69/245), and every "38 distinct scenarios" support figure
  (`n_items`/`calls`/`coverage`) is reported **without** its merged evidence, understating support.
  (b) has **zero effect in the recorded runs**: I checked all 69 (min 16), 65 (turn-aware) and 11
  (min 50) `merge_into_key` values and every one resolves to an accepted `scenario_key`.
  Worth noting separately: the 69 merges land on only **22 distinct targets**, so
  "38 distinct scenarios enriched by 69 merged duplicates" is loose — 16 of the 38 received nothing.
- **Confidence**: certain that the code diverges; speculative on the magnitude of (a).
- **Cheapest way to verify**: diff `trial_adjudicate_gemini.py:300-319` against
  `v2/layer_a.py:504-532`.

#### 10. Blind-judge batches are contiguous size bands, with no interleaving and no double-judged cluster

- **File:line**: `Brain/calibration/export_cluster_batches.py:146-148`
  (`chunk = blind[start:start + a.batch]` over a list already sorted largest-first at `:95`).
- **What is wrong**: batch 00 is the 41 largest clusters (69-822 turns), batch 05 the 40 smallest
  (16-19 turns) — verified from the exported files. Each batch went to a different subagent, so
  **judge identity is perfectly confounded with cluster size**, and the stated rationale for
  batching ("see them RELATIVE to each other") means each judge calibrated against a
  size-homogeneous band. Zero clusters appear in two batches (verified), so there is no
  inter-rater reliability measurement at all — this repo's other judge harnesses (head-to-head C1
  position-swap, skills judge null) all carry one.
- **What number it corrupts, and in WHICH DIRECTION**: the judge coachability rate (92/245) and
  therefore the agreement rate. Per-batch judge-yes ranges 26.8%-43.9%, but Gemma-retained moves
  with it (29.3%-51.2%) in the same bands, so there is **no evidence of a large judge-leniency
  effect** — the design flaw is real, its measured impact is small. Direction unknown.
- **Confidence**: certain about the design; likely that the impact is minor given the tracking.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: min/max `n_turns` per `artifacts/cluster_batches/min16_batch*.json`.

### CLEAN

- **`trial_gateway.py` — taxonomy item 10 (silent drop), the headline hazard: genuinely clean.**
  Every path to `/embeddings` is count-checked. `embed_one` (`:271-283`) asserts exactly one
  vector for one text *and* asserts the returned width; `embed()` (`:296-312`) calls only
  `embed_one`, pre-allocates a positional `out` list (never `zip`s two lists) and raises if any
  slot is unfilled; `embed_batched` (`:325-334`) compares `len(got) != len(texts)` and falls back
  to per-text, returning a `batch_worked` flag. `grep -n '"/embeddings"'` finds exactly those two
  call sites. The self-test at `:466-478` additionally verifies the batched output
  **positionally** against independently embedded references, which is stronger than the count
  check that failed silently in the native-SDK incident. I found no `zip()` in any of the four
  files that could truncate a length mismatch.
- **`trial_gateway.py` — item 5 (a check that cannot fail).** The batch-collapse check at
  `:451-456` was deliberately converted from an assertion into a `note` precisely because
  asserting the bug reproduces is itself a defect; the comment says so. Correct as written.
- **Embedding cache keying (items 11/12).** `trial_pool_unit_gemini.py:122-123` keys on
  `sha256(model | NATIVE_DIMS | text)`. Model ✓, native width ✓ (not the `--width` analysis
  width — the trap is explicitly avoided and documented at `:76-79`), text ✓. Task type is
  correctly absent: `embed_one` never sends one, and `trial_gateway` measured `task_type` inert on
  this backend (cosine 1.000000 with and without). Duplicate texts collapse to one key and get
  identical vectors, which is correct. I could not construct a case where a cheap run poisons the
  cache with wrong-width vectors — the width is inside the key.
- **Item 4 (name that never matches) for the decision enum.** `_KIND_BY_DECISION`'s keys
  (`new_scenario`, `mechanics`, `not_coachable`) are exactly the strings
  `PROMPT_LAYER_A_V2_TRIAGE` (`shared/prompts.py:850-860`) instructs the model to emit, and
  `merge_into` is intentionally absent from the dict and special-cased at
  `trial_adjudicate_gemini.py:304/316`. Empirically all 245/76/245 rows carry one of the four
  decisions, zero fell through to the `KIND_SCENARIO` default. `ver[i]["coachable"] == "yes"` /
  `["coherent"] == "yes"` match the literals present in every verdict file (`yes`/`no`, plus
  `partial` for coherent only). One residual: neither comparison guards against an unexpected
  value, so a future judge writing `"Yes"` or `"partial"` for coachable would be silently binned
  as *not* coachable — latent, zero occurrences today.
- **Item 3 (numbering/base mismatch).** `cluster_id` is `r["i"]`, the 0-based adjudication index,
  written by `trial_adjudicate_gemini.py:303`, exported unchanged at
  `export_cluster_batches.py:137`, and joined back at `aggregate_cluster_verdicts.py:44/51`.
  Verified end to end: the six min-16 batch files span ids 0-244 contiguously with no gaps or
  duplicates, and all 245 are judged. No off-by-one anywhere in this chain.
- **Item 10 for the join itself.** `aggregate_cluster_verdicts.py:62-65` computes and prints
  `missing`, and `load()` would overwrite a duplicated `cluster_id` — I checked, there are none
  across all nine verdict files, and coverage is 245/245 and 76/76.
- **Item 15 (hardcoded count dressed as a formula).** `min_cluster_size=16` is passed as an
  explicit override to `fit_topic_model`, is documented as "a count, not a cosine", and *is*
  genuinely scale-matched: 16/23,949 = 0.067% vs clause mode's 50/73,771 = 0.068%. The
  presentation constants (`NEAREST_SHOWN=3`, `REPRESENTATIVE_SHOWN=6`, `SAMPLES_PER_CLUSTER=12`)
  are prompt-layout, not thresholds.
- **Item 6 (a null that is not matched).** There is no null, Monte-Carlo or otherwise, in any of
  these four files — the size-matched account null lives in `flag_proper_noun_clusters.py`, which
  is outside this batch. Nothing to check here.
- **Published counts I verified and found CORRECT**: 245 min-16 clusters ✓; retention
  107/245 = 43.7% of clusters ✓ and 5,159/12,582 = 41.0% of *clustered* turns ✓ (but see finding 4
  for the denominator); 69 merge_into decisions ✓; "of 138 genuinely sunk clusters judges want
  ZERO" ✓ (129 mechanics + 9 logistics, all judged `coachable: no`); "15 clusters Gemma kept that
  judges would drop" ✓ (2 scenario + 13 merged); three measures agreeing — proxy 97/245 = 39.6%
  subject-bearing ✓, blind judges 92/245 = 37.6% coachable ✓, Gemma retention 43.7% ✓;
  Matryoshka and one-vector-per-request are implemented as documented ✓.
- **Not verifiable from code or artifacts, flagged as unchecked**: whether the nine judging
  subagents were distinct or shared, and what prompt they were given — no judge prompt, model id
  or `judged_by` field is recorded anywhere in `cluster_batches/`. CLAUDE.md's own rule
  ("Model provenance is recorded per verdict … do this in any future judge harness") is not
  followed on the judge side of this experiment. `trial_adjudicate_gemini.py` does record
