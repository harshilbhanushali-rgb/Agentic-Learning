### FINDINGS

#### 1. DEFECT 1 confirmed and quantified — 45.8% of client turns are structurally unscoreable, and 98.7% of `Signal_Recognition_Failure` is the segmenter, not a CSM

- **File:line**: `ego_trap/transcript_parser.py:78-89` (`turns_until_next_client`), `:92-104`
  (`classify_response_outcome`), consumed at `ego_trap/signal_check.py:240` and `:339`.
- **What is wrong**: `turns_until_next_client` iterates `turns[start_index + 1:]` and `break`s
  on the first `CLIENT` turn. When `turns[start_index + 1]` is itself CLIENT — a pause, a
  continued thought, or the transcriber splitting one utterance — the window is `[]` and
  `classify_response_outcome` returns `"none"` **before any evidence is consulted**. There is
  no counter and no warning distinguishing this from real silence.
- **Measured over all 106 mapped CSM transcripts** (6,468 CLIENT turns):

  | outcome | n |
  | --- | --- |
  | `csm` | 2,351 |
  | `other_joveo` | 1,117 |
  | `none` | **3,000** |

  Of the 3,000 `none`: **2,962 (98.7%) are immediately followed by another CLIENT turn**,
  38 are the last turn of the call, and **0 are genuine silence**. This reproduces the
  recorded 98.6% on a slightly larger corpus.
- **What number it corrupts, and in which direction**:
  - `signal_recognition_gaps.missed` and `.occurrences` — **inflated by ~98.7% of their
    `missed` mass**. The `39/61 (64%) missed` figure quoted in `ego_trap/RUN_NOTES.md:34` is
    this artifact. Nothing in the codebase READS this table (grep confirms: written only), so
    the corruption reaches humans through hand-written SQL, which is why it has survived.
  - Every `gap_events` row with `gap_type = "Signal_Recognition_Failure"` — inflated by the
    same factor. Any coaching output derived from them is fabricated.
  - **A second consequence not in the recorded write-up: the SCORED population is a biased
    subset.** Only a client turn that is *last in its contiguous client block* can ever reach
    `outcome == "csm"`, so **45.8% of client turns can never be scored at all, by
    construction** — regardless of what the CSM did. This is taxonomy item 2 and item 13.
    I measured the direction rather than assuming it: excluded turns are **shorter** (median
    17 words vs 25, mean 32.6 vs 39.4), so the bias is mild and runs *toward* excluding
    filler, not toward excluding substance. It still means `milestone_performance.attempts`
    is drawn from a non-random half of the corpus and is **understated by roughly a factor of
    two** relative to "moments a client raised something".
  - Also: `signal["client_utterance"]` carries only the block-final turn, so if the situated
    grader is ever switched on it will see a fragment of the client's move. Currently inert
    (`situated_fields` is not passed in `pipeline.py`).
- **Confidence**: certain (mechanism read from code; counts measured).
- **Cheapest way to verify**: re-run the 25-line counter I used, or by hand —
  `ego_trap/transcript_parser.py:85-88`: the loop body `if t.role == CLIENT: break` executes
  on the very first iteration whenever the next turn is CLIENT, so `result` is `[]` and both
  `any(...)` tests at `:100`/`:102` are False.

#### 2. `signal_recognition_gaps` counts `recognized` and `missed` over DIFFERENT populations

- **File:line**: `ego_trap/gap_output.py:184` (`recognized=True`) vs `:214`
  (`recognized=False`), gated by `ego_trap/pipeline.py:260-261` and `:176-185`.
- **What is wrong**: `recognized=True` is written **only** from `write_gap_record`, which is
  reached only for a signal that (a) had a `csm` outcome, (b) had a rubric in `rubrics`, and
  (c) survived a successful Gemma batch (`if "milestone_results" not in item: continue`).
  `recognized=False` is written from `write_signal_recognition_failure`, which is called for
  **every** coachable `none` signal with **no rubric requirement and no Gemma dependency**.
  `write_rubric_coverage_gap` and `write_deferred_to_teammate` touch neither counter (both
  documented and correct in isolation) — but the asymmetry is that only the *recognized* side
  carries the rubric filter.
- **What number it corrupts, and in which direction**: the recognition rate
  `recognized / occurrences` is **deflated**, on two independent axes. Concretely: a coachable
  scenario with `rubric_status = 'skipped_insufficient_responses'` (kept in `coachable_map`
  deliberately — `ego_trap/scenario_pool.py:37-42`) can only ever accumulate `missed`, so its
  `signal_recognition_gaps` row reads **100% missed no matter how well the CSM performed**.
  Stacked on top of finding 1, the published "64% missed" is doubly wrong.
- **Confidence**: certain.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: grep `upsert_signal_recognition_gap` — it has exactly two call
  sites, and only one of them sits behind the rubric lookup.

#### 3. A mixed CSM/teammate response window is graded as if the CSM said all of it — while the CSM is handed only their own fragment

- **File:line**: `ego_trap/rubric_lookup.py:91-94` (`extract_csm_response_window`), against
  `ego_trap/transcript_parser.py:100-103`.
- **What is wrong**: `classify_response_outcome` returns `"csm"` if **any** CSM turn appears
  in the window, but `extract_csm_response_window` then joins **only** the CSM turns, dropping
  every `OTHER_JOVEO` turn. So when a teammate gives the substantive answer and the CSM says
  "yeah, exactly", the exchange is *not* reclassified as `Deferred_To_Teammate` (that only
  fires when there is **no** CSM turn at all), and the CSM's fragment is scored against the
  full rubric. The `Deferred_To_Teammate` protection has a hole exactly where it is most
  needed.
- **Measured over the 2,351 scored (`csm`) windows in the same corpus**:
  - **408 (17.4%) are mixed** CSM + teammate.
  - In **158** of those the CSM contributed **<34% of the words** — the teammate gave most of
    the answer.
  - **384 windows (16.3% of all scored windows) contain ≤5 CSM words in total** and are graded
    against a rubric averaging ~6 milestones.
  - Median CSM word share inside a mixed window: 0.51. Median CSM words in a scored window: 39.
- **What number it corrupts, and in which direction**: `milestone_performance.hits` /
  `partial_hits` are **deflated** and `milestones_missed` **inflated**, unambiguously — a
  5-word acknowledgement cannot satisfy a criterion. This is a direct, unexamined contributor
  to the ~9% `W(matched)` level that the whole profile effort has been trying to explain, and
  unlike wording it is a *population* defect, so no prompt change touches it.
- **Confidence**: certain (mechanism), certain (rates; measured on real transcripts).
- **Cheapest way to verify**: read `ego_trap/rubric_lookup.py:94` — the `if t.role ==
  EgoTrapRole.CSM` filter — next to `ego_trap/transcript_parser.py:100`, which does not
  require the CSM to be the *only* responder.

#### 4. A failed Gemma batch marks the transcript checkpoint DONE — permanent, unrecoverable loss

- **File:line**: `ego_trap/pipeline.py:249-251` (`except GemmaError: ... continue`) and
  `:276` (`checkpoint.mark_done(run_id, stem, layer)`), unconditional.
- **What is wrong**: the `continue` skips the batch loop iteration, not the transcript. The
  transcript then falls through Stage 5 (which correctly skips items lacking
  `milestone_results`) and reaches `mark_done` regardless. On resume,
  `checkpoint.is_done(...)` at `:108` returns True and the lost signals are never revisited.
- **What number it corrupts, and in which direction**:
  - `milestone_performance.attempts` — **deflated** by exactly the batch size × milestones per
    rubric, silently and permanently.
  - `signal_recognition_gaps.recognized` — **deflated** (see finding 2); `missed` is untouched,
    so the recognition rate moves down.
  - `gap_events` — rows simply absent, with no marker of the hole.
  - On a **forced re-run without `ops/clear_ego_trap_data.py`**, the direction inverts for the
    successful signals: `attempts = attempts + 1` double-counts them while the previously
    failed ones are counted once, so `W = (hits + 0.5·partial)/attempts` is distorted by a
    *non-uniform* denominator. That non-uniformity is worse than a uniform doubling, because
    it cannot be corrected by dividing by two.
  - The printed `[Stage 5/5] {written}/{len(scoreable)}` line *does* surface the shortfall in
    the log, so this is not undetectable — but nothing in the DB records it.
- **Confidence**: certain.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: `ego_trap/pipeline.py` — there is no `try/except` around the
  per-transcript body and no flag threaded from the batch loop to line 276.

#### 5. Soft-skill scoring has none of the truncation guards milestone scoring has, clamps silently, and runs on a different model

- **File:line**: `ego_trap/milestone_scoring.py:452-497` (`score_soft_skills_batch`),
  `:122-147` (`_normalize_rating`), consumed at `ego_trap/gap_output.py:141-148`.
  Live: `tuning.yaml` `layer_d.score_soft_skills: true`.
- **What is wrong**, four things in one function, each of which the milestone path explicitly
  defends against:
  1. **No missing-id report.** `score_milestones_batch:333-347` computes `expected - set(by_id)`
     and shouts. `score_soft_skills_batch` does not. A truncated JSON array is invisible.
  2. **The clamp is silent in the case that actually happens.** `_normalize_rating` warns only
     `if rating is not None`. A missing id yields `r = {}` → `raw.get("rating")` is `None` →
     **returns `"adequate"` with no print**. The warning fires only for a *wrong* string, which
     is the rarer case; the documented behaviour ("warns rather than silently clamping") holds
     for a wrong value and **fails for a missing one**.
  3. **Default output cap.** `call_gemma(prompt, config.gemma_api_keys)` at `:485` takes the
     8192 default, not the `_SCORING_MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS = 16384` the milestone call passes —
     i.e. the more truncation-prone path is the one with no truncation detector.
  4. **Different model.** `:485` also takes `gemma.py`'s `_DEFAULT_MODEL =
     "gemini-3.5-flash-lite"` with the default fallback chain, while milestones are pinned to
     `_SCORING_MODEL = "gemini-3.1-flash-lite"`. So within a single scored exchange the two
     halves are graded by two different models, and `scored_by` is recorded on milestone
     results but **not** on soft-skill results.
- **What number it corrupts, and in which direction**: `gap_events` `Soft_Skill_Failure`
  counts are **deflated**, because the clamp target (`"adequate"`) is the one value that emits
  no gap (`gap_output.py:142`). A truncated batch converts every failure in the dropped tail
  into a pass. **The clamped rows are not distinguishable in the output at all** — no field,
  no gap row, no log line — so the deflation is unmeasurable after the fact. This is the exact
  inverse of the milestone path, where a dropped id becomes a `miss` and therefore over-reports
  failure; the two halves of the same batch fail in opposite directions.
- **Confidence**: certain (code), certain (flag is on).
- **Cheapest way to verify**: diff `score_milestones_batch:318-347` against
  `score_soft_skills_batch:484-496` — the `missing`/`unexpected` block and the `model=` /
  `max_output_tokens=` arguments are simply absent from the second.

#### 6. `Unknown Speaker` turns are classified as CLIENT; only an operator flag keeps them out

- **File:line**: `ego_trap/transcript_parser.py:23-30` (`_classify` falls through to
  `EgoTrapRole.CLIENT`), `ego_trap/pipeline.py:120` (no roster argument).
- **What is wrong**: Layer D's classifier is fail-open, and **`ego_trap/` never reads the
  `.speakers.json` sidecars at all** — 103 of them sit next to the CSM transcripts and are
  used by `v1/pipeline.py`, `v2/pipeline.py` and eight calibration harnesses, but
  `ego_trap/transcript_parser.parse_transcript` has no `roster` parameter.
- **Measured**: with the current 32-name `JOVEO_SPEAKER_NAMES`, **0 of 254 `@joveo.com`
  speaker slots across the 103 sidecars fall through to CLIENT** — the roster backfill work
  closed that hole (see CLEAN below). What remains is **417 turns labelled
  `Unknown Speaker`, all classified CLIENT**, plus one transcript with no `mapping.csv` row.
- **What number it corrupts, and in which direction**: signal count and therefore
  `attempts` / `occurrences` are **inflated** by up to 417 unattributable turns. This is
  currently suppressed only by `ops/run_ego_trap.py --exclude` being passed by hand; nothing
  in the code refuses an unattributable speaker, so the next unattended run reintroduces it.
- **Confidence**: certain (mechanism and counts). The 417 figure is the exposure, not the
  realised error, since the last run excluded those files.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: `grep -c "^Unknown Speaker$" csm_recordings/*.txt`, and
  `grep -rn "load_roster" ego_trap/` → no hits.

#### 7. `segmenter`'s `len(sent) < 4` is a hardcoded token count that is decided by PUNCTUATION

- **File:line**: `preprocessing/segmenter.py:23`.
- **What is wrong**: `len(sent)` is spaCy's **token** count, which includes punctuation. So the
  de-facto posture filter is:
  `"That makes sense."` → 4 tokens → **kept**; `"Got it."` → 3 → dropped; `"No."` → 2 →
  dropped; but **`"Yeah, sure."` → `Yeah / , / sure / .` → 4 → kept** and `"Right."` → 2 →
  dropped. Two two-word backchannels get opposite verdicts purely on whether the transcriber
  wrote a comma. It is also a hardcoded count (taxonomy item 15): it does not scale with
  anything and freezes at 4 for any corpus.
- **What number it corrupts, and in which direction**: the Layer A clause pool's content-free
  share (measured at 59.1%) and every count derived from it, **inflated** by the
  comma-bearing backchannel that survives. Shared with `v2/layer_c.py:77`, so the same cutoff
  silently governs the milestone clause pool — changing it moves both, which is exactly why
  it has never been touched.
- **Confidence**: certain for the mechanism; the *magnitude* of the punctuation-driven
  admission is unmeasured (speculative as a quantity).
- **Cheapest way to verify**: `nlp("Yeah, sure.")` vs `nlp("Got it.")` and compare `len(...)`.

#### 8. Minor: `get_milestone_gap_profile`'s INNER JOIN silently drops rows, and the label cache is keyed wrong

- **File:line**: `shared/storage.py:410-411` (`JOIN rubrics r ON r.rubric_id = mp.rubric_id`),
  `ego_trap/pipeline.py:285-288`, `ego_trap/gap_output.py:60-66`.
- **What is wrong**: (a) the join is inner, so any `milestone_performance` row whose
  `rubric_id` no longer exists after a Layer C re-run **vanishes from the gap profile with no
  counter** (taxonomy item 10). (b) `pipeline.py:287` fetches the rubric by
  `r["scenario_key"]` but caches it under `r["rubric_id"]`; if two rows share a `rubric_id`
  with different `scenario_key` payloads, the first one seen wins and every later row is
  labelled from the wrong rubric's milestone array.
- **What number it corrupts, and in which direction**: (a) the printed profile row count is
  **deflated**; the underlying counters are untouched. (b) only the human-readable `label`
  column of the printed report, not any number.
- **Confidence**: certain for (a) as a mechanism; (b) is likely but I did not find a live case.
- **Cheapest way to verify**: `SELECT count(*) FROM milestone_performance mp LEFT JOIN rubrics
  r USING (rubric_id) WHERE r.rubric_id IS NULL;` — **do not run it as part of this audit**;
  noted for whoever fixes it.

### CLEAN

Each of these was checked specifically and is correct. Stated so the next person does not
re-check them.

- **Taxonomy item 4 (name that never matches) — clean across the whole batch.** I grepped every
  string literal compared against an enum, a DB value or a dict key, and confirmed each against
  its definition: `"csm"` / `"other_joveo"` / `"none"` are exactly the three returns of
  `classify_response_outcome:100-104` and exactly the three compared at `pipeline.py:148-150`;
  `"full_hit"` / `"partial_hit"` / `"miss"` match `_VALID_VERDICTS` and the
  `upsert_milestone_performance` branch; `"failing"` at `gap_output.py:142` is in
  `_VALID_RATINGS`; `turn_match_mode: normalized` is in `_TURN_MATCH_MODES`;
  `signal_detection_mode` is checked with an explicit `raise` on an unknown value;
  `not_coachable_flag`, `detection_hint`, `soft_skill_rubric`, `is_coachable`, `rubric_status`,
  `soft_skills` all match their writers. The known `"OTHER_JOVEO"` vs `JOVEO_OTHER` bug is
  confined to `ego_trap/call_scoring.py` (shipped OFF, outside this batch) and is already
  documented there at `:207-208`.
- **Taxonomy item 3 (numbering base) — clean.** `EgoTrapTurn.index` is 0-based
  (`index=len(turns)` at `:70`), `find_turn_index` returns `t.index` unmodified,
  `signal["turn_index"]` is passed verbatim into `turns_until_next_client`,
  `extract_csm_response_window` and `gap_events.signal_turn_index`, and the pipeline's own
  `@ turn N` print uses the same 0-based value. There is no display/storage boundary that
  shifts it. The only 1-based numbering in the batch is `milestone_ids` → `f"M{i+1}"`, and
  `format_gap_profile:61` correctly reverses it with `int(...lstrip("M")) - 1` (verified for
  `M3` → index 2 and `M10` → index 9).
- **`embed_query` vs `embed_document` — clean at every call site in this batch.**
  `signal_check._score:83-85` embeds client turns with `embed_query_matrix` and scenarios with
  `build_scenario_vecs` → `embed_document` — the same query/document split
  `layer_b.assign_scenarios` was calibrated on, so Layer D reproduces the trigger-vs-scenario
  band rather than a third one. `rank_benchmark_responses:58-61` uses `embed_document_matrix`
  on both sides (responses and `scenario_vec`), which is document-vs-document and matches the
  separately-measured response-vs-scenario band. No call site mixes them.
- **`_matrix` and list variants provably agree.** `embed_query` is literally
  `_embed_matrix(texts, _QUERY_PREFIX).tolist()` and `embed_query_matrix` is
  `_embed_matrix(texts, _QUERY_PREFIX)` — one code path, one cache key, no possibility of
  divergence (`embedder.py:409-420`).
- **Speaker fail-open is real but currently NOT firing for Joveo staff — measured, not
  assumed.** Across all 103 `csm_recordings/*.speakers.json`, **254 of 254 speaker slots with a
  `@joveo.com` email** are matched either by the mapped `csm_name` or by one of the 32 names in
  `JOVEO_SPEAKER_NAMES`; **zero** fall through to CLIENT. Resulting split over 106 mapped
  transcripts: 6,468 CLIENT / 3,563 CSM / 2,573 OTHER_JOVEO turns. The only residue is finding
  6 (`Unknown Speaker`). So the "internal chatter becomes coaching findings" risk is closed for
  the current roster — it is a maintenance hazard (a new colleague not added to the env var),
  not a live corruption.
- **`preprocessing/transcript_parser._classify`'s roster path is correct on the live corpus.**
  Line 55 requires `s == name_l` **exactly** before returning `SpeakerRole.NAREN`, while every
  other role is resolved by prefix match — so a transcript spelling the name differently from
  Avoma's roster would silently demote Naren to `JOVEO_OTHER` and zero out
  `layer_b.extract_pairs` for that call. Checked: `NAREN_SPEAKER_NAME=Naren Shankar`, the
  rosters carry `"Naren Shankar"`, and **13,222 of 13,222** Naren speaker lines across
  `recordings/` are the exact string `Naren Shankar`. Latent trap, zero current effect — do not
  spend on it, but do not loosen the roster name source either.
- **`rank_benchmark_responses`'s `len(responses) <= limit` short-circuit (taxonomy item 9) is
  harmless in production.** It returns rows unranked and without `scenario_similarity`, which
  is what broke the dry-run harness's chosen-vs-discarded band — but the only production caller
  (`get_benchmark_reference:88`) joins `response_text` only and never reads the score, and when
  the branch fires it is returning *all* the rows anyway, so no ranking decision is skipped.
- **`insert_gap_event` tolerates the three writers that omit `milestones_partial_hit`.**
  `write_signal_recognition_failure`, `write_deferred_to_teammate` and
  `write_rubric_coverage_gap` all omit the key; `storage.py:482` uses
  `event.get("milestones_partial_hit", [])`, so it stores `[]`, not NULL. No KeyError, no
  NULL-vs-empty ambiguity downstream.
- **`embedder.py`'s hosted-Gemini path is dormant and I did not audit it for number-moving
  defects.** `tuning.yaml` ships `embedding.backend: local` and `cache_enabled: true`, so
  `_encode_gemini`, `_pick_key`, `_park` and the task-type mapping never execute in any
  production or Layer D path. The one thing worth carrying forward if that ever changes: the
  module's own comment at `:120-131` records that `task_type` is **inert** on
  `gemini-embedding-2`, so `embed_query` and `embed_document` would return **identical**
  vectors on that backend — the query/document asymmetry every threshold in `tuning.yaml` was
  calibrated against disappears entirely rather than merely shifting.
- **`select_signal` / `topk_pick` sink rejection is consistent.** `select_signal:139-142`
  rejects on `np.argmax` over the **full** row (sinks included) before delegating, and
  `topk_pick` then ranks only non-sinks — so a turn whose global best is a sink can never be
  admitted through the margin. That is the intended asymmetry with `flat_pick`, and it is
  implemented correctly.
