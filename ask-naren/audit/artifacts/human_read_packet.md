# Ask Naren — human read (issue #9)

**12 items. Please judge each one, then send me the verdicts** (just `1 wrong`, `2 right`, … is
plenty; a one-line reason where you have one is a bonus).

## Why you specifically

Two model instruments disagree sharply about these same items, and asking a third model
cannot resolve it. One says ~80% of Ask Naren's answers are right. The other says the
retrieved source answers a *different question* in 84% of exactly those "right" cases. Your
verdicts tell us which one is miscalibrated — and if it's the first, the 80% headline we have
been building on is wrong.

**Some of these 12 were picked because the two disagree, and some because they agree. Which
is which is deliberately not shown.** Please judge each on its own merits.

## What you are looking at

- **SITUATION** — what a client said. A CSM needs to respond to this.
- **RETRIEVED — client said / Naren replied** — the past exchange the tool picked as the
  closest match, and what Naren really said there. This is **spoken transcript**: fragmentary,
  unpolished, sometimes trailing off mid-sentence. Judge it as speech, not writing.
- **ANSWER** — what the tool produced for the CSM to use.

## The one question

**Is this answer right?** It is **right** when both hold:

1. it addresses the situation the CSM is actually facing, and
2. it is supported by what Naren really said — it paraphrases or applies that reply, rather
   than adding substance the reply does not contain.

It is **wrong** if either fails. The case worth catching: an answer that is fluent, confident
and correctly quoted, but answers a **different question** than the situation asks, because
the retrieved exchange was about something adjacent. Fluency is not evidence. Nor is an
accurate quote.

Judge the answer **as delivered**. Don't credit it for being close to something right, and
don't penalise it for being brief, plainly worded, or for leaving out things the retrieved
reply also left out.

Use **uncertain** only if the situation itself is too fragmentary to tell what was being
asked — not as a hedge on a hard call.
